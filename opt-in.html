The business and the use case

CONSEQ is an iOS app for adults trying to quit a compulsive habit. The user restricts their own phone using Apple's Screen Time system and names one person — an "accountability contact" — who is notified if the user breaks the rules they set for themselves.

That notification is the only message this program sends. There is no marketing, no promotion, no newsletter, and no second message type. The app has no accounts, sells nothing, and shares no data with third parties other than its messaging provider.

There is exactly one opt-in path

SMS opt-in only. There is no website form, no keyword advertised on signage or in print, no QR code, no paper form, and no verbal opt-in. The call to action is delivered inside a single SMS message, reproduced verbatim in Step 3 below.

Step 1 — inside the app

The user names one person and states their relationship to them

An existing CONSEQ user enters one mobile number and a first name. Before anything is sent, the app tells the user, in these words, that the recipient has not agreed to anything yet:

[Name] has to agree first. They never installed this app and never agreed to anything. So we ask them, and they say yes — or they don't and nothing gets sent to them, ever.

The user is also required to supply their own first name, because an unidentified text is indistinguishable from spam. The app states: "[Name] is about to get a text from a number they don't recognise. It needs to say who's asking, or they'll ignore it — and they'd be right to."

Step 2 — inside the app

The user is shown the exact message and must actively send it

The full text of the message that will be sent is displayed on screen, verbatim, before it is sent. The user must deliberately tap to send it. Nothing is pre-checked, nothing is pre-selected, and no message is sent as a side effect of any other action in the app.

Step 3 — the call to action

The recipient receives one message containing the CTA

This is the only message sent to a number that has not opted in, and it is sent once:

[Sam] added you as their accountability contact in CONSEQ, an app for quitting a habit. You'd get a text only when they break their own rules — a few times a month at most. Reply YES to agree. Reply STOP to opt out at any time. Msg & data rates may apply.

The message identifies the sending program by name (CONSEQ), identifies the person who initiated it, describes the message type and frequency, and carries both the opt-out instruction and the rates disclosure.

Step 4 — consent

The recipient replies YES, and only then are they enrolled

Consent is an affirmative inbound reply from the recipient's own handset. Accepted keywords: YES, Y, AGREE, ACCEPT, OK, OKAY.

The inbound reply is recorded as the consent record: the phone number, the verbatim message body, the timestamp, and the carrier message SID. This record is retained as proof of opt-in.

Automatic reply confirming enrollment

CONSEQ: You're set. You'll only hear from us if the person who added you breaks their own rules — a few times a month at most. Reply HELP for help, STOP to opt out. Msg & data rates may apply.
Step 5 — after opt-in

What the recipient then receives

A short notification, only when the person who added them reports breaking a commitment they made, asks to disable the app's protections, or removes those protections outside the app. Every such message is signed and carries opt-out instructions:

it's me. I broke the protocol i set, [12] days into my run. you're the person i picked to find out. you don't have to do anything with this. i wanted it to cost something, and this is the cost. — sent via CONSEQ. Reply STOP to opt out.

Consent is active, never assumed

  • No pre-checked boxes exist anywhere in this flow. Consent is an affirmative SMS reply typed by the recipient.
  • Silence is not consent. An unanswered invitation expires after 48 hours.
  • A number may be invited a maximum of 3 times, ever, across all users of the app.
  • Replying NO or STOP is a permanent refusal. That number is never invited again by anyone and can never be messaged.
  • Consent is not transferable. It is stored against the phone number itself, not against the user who submitted it.
  • The messaging service refuses, at the server, to send to any number without a recorded affirmative opt-in. This is enforced in code, not by policy alone.

What this program never does

  • No marketing or promotional messages of any kind.
  • No links or phone numbers in message bodies.
  • No purchased, rented, or third-party-sourced phone number lists.
  • No sharing of phone numbers with anyone except the messaging provider that delivers the message.
  • No message to any number that has not replied with an opt-in keyword.
