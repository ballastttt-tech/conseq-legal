CONSEQ Privacy Policy

Last updated: 6 August 2026 · Contact: ballast.ttt@gmail.com · Provider: CONSEQ

CONSEQ helps you restrict your own phone. Nothing you put into the app leaves the phone you put it on. This policy says what the app keeps and where it keeps it.

We collect nothing about you. There is no account, no sign in and no server. The app has no way to send your information anywhere. The only thing we ever see is an email you decide to write to us, and there is a section below on exactly what that means.

What the app keeps on your phone

Everything you tell CONSEQ stays on the phone you typed it into. There is no account and no record of you on any server.

The app writes these to a file on your phone:

  • Your age.
  • What you picked when it asked what you want back.
  • What you picked when it asked what you had already tried.
  • The total of your answers to the five questions about your own behaviour, and which of four groups that total puts you in.
  • Your answers to the two questions that come after those, kept as the numbers you picked, because the app reads them to decide what to say to you next.
  • One line the app writes for you out of what you said you wanted back. It shows that line back to you later. You do not type it, and in this version you cannot edit it.
  • The day your run started, and the day you accepted the terms.
  • Your log. That means every check in you made, every panic session you finished or walked away from, every time you told the app you broke your own rules, and every time a block you had asked for stopped you.
  • Whether you granted Screen Time access.

Two more things are kept on the phone by the system, for the app:

  • Which apps and categories you chose to block, and the daily time you allowed each one. Apple hands these to the app as sealed tokens. The next section explains what that means.
  • The wait you chose before the Protocol page can be edited.

Your individual answers to the five questions are never written to disk. Only the total, and the group that total falls into. Which boxes you ticked is gone the moment setup ends.

About those questions

They are CONSEQ's own questions. The number they add up to is only there to decide which words the app shows you next. It is not a test result, not a rating of how bad anything is, and not a diagnosis. Nobody gets diagnosed by an app.

Screen Time and blocking

CONSEQ applies its restrictions through Apple's Family Controls, Managed Settings and Device Activity systems. You grant Screen Time access once, and everything after that happens on your own phone.

Four things happen there:

  • Sites meant for adults are filtered out, in every browser on the phone.
  • The apps and categories you picked are blocked.
  • Any of those can have a daily time limit you set. When the time is used up, that app is blocked for the rest of the day.
  • If you switched it on, apps cannot be deleted from the phone while CONSEQ is running. That covers every app on the phone, not only the ones you picked. Apple's system offers no narrower version of it.

We cannot see what you browse or which apps you use. Apple designs that system so an app like this one is handed sealed identifiers that mean nothing outside your phone. We never learn which apps they are, and we are never told which sites you visited or tried to visit. That information is not available to us at any price.

You can take Screen Time access away at any time in the iOS Settings app. That clears every restriction CONSEQ applied, including the one on deleting apps.

The camera and the motion sensor

Two of the panic tasks use the camera, and you choose whether to start one.

The breathing task turns on the front camera and shows you your own face for sixty seconds while you breathe. Nothing is attached to that picture at all: no photo, no video, no file, and nothing reading it.

The object task turns on the back camera and names one ordinary thing to go and point it at: a fridge, your shoes, a couch or a microwave. Your phone works out what it is looking at using Apple's Vision framework, which runs entirely on the phone. Frames are read and dropped in the same instant. No image is saved, and no image is sent anywhere, because the app has nowhere to send one.

iOS asks you for camera permission the first time one of these starts. You can say no. If you do, the other panic tasks still work.

The shake task reads the phone's motion sensor. That needs no permission, and the readings are counted and dropped as they arrive.

If you email us

The Settings tab has a Get support button and a Request a feature button. Both of them open your own Mail app with a message addressed to ballast.ttt@gmail.com, already filled in with the app's version number, its build number and your iOS version. You write the rest, and you decide whether to send it.

If you do send it, we receive your email address and whatever you wrote, the same as with any other email. We read it to answer you and we use it for nothing else. Nothing from inside the app is attached to it, and the app cannot send one on its own.

What leaves your phone

Nothing. The app never contacts a server, and it never hands what it holds to anyone else. There is no analytics kit in it, no crash reporter, and no code from any other company that could send something on its behalf. The app does not ask for permission to send you alerts, and it never sends one.

Two links inside the app open your browser: this policy and the terms. Opening a web page is your browser's business, and nothing about you goes with it.

The one exception is the one you make yourself. If you tap a support button and then send the email your Mail app opens, that email reaches us, because you sent it. Nothing else ever does.

We never sell what you tell us

We hold nothing about you, so there is nothing for us to sell or to share, and we would not do either in any case. There are no advertising partners, no data brokers, and no other recipients of any kind. That covers support email too: we do not pass it on, and we do not use it to advertise anything to you.

There is no advertising in this app, no analytics kit, and no tracking of any kind.

What we keep, and for how long

Nothing, unless you emailed us. There is no record of you anywhere but your own phone, because the app never sends one. Everything the app holds lives on your phone for exactly as long as you keep it there, and goes when you erase it.

Support email is the one thing held anywhere else. We keep those messages in an ordinary mailbox for as long as we need them to answer you, and no longer than 12 months, then we delete them. Ask us to delete yours sooner and we will.

Deleting everything

Inside the app: open the Protocol tab, scroll to The way out, and tap Erase everything and start over. You then hold a button to confirm. That lifts every restriction CONSEQ applied and deletes your answers, your run and your whole log from the phone. The app returns to how it was the first time you opened it.

Because CONSEQ keeps everything on your phone, that one action removes everything the app has. The only thing it cannot reach is an email you sent us, which sits in our mailbox rather than in the app. Write to us and we will delete that too.

Children

CONSEQ is for adults. It is rated 18+ and is not directed at anyone under 18, and we do not knowingly collect information from children.

The app asks your age and will not let you pick a number below 18. That is your own answer and we have no way to check it. If someone under 18 emails us, we delete the message once we know.

Your rights

Depending on where you live you may have the right to see, correct, delete or export what we hold, and to object to how we use it. We hold nothing about you except an email if you sent one, so the erase option above covers everything in the app, and asking us covers the rest. We will not treat you any differently for asking. Email ballast.ttt@gmail.com with any question about this.

Changes

If this policy changes materially we will update the date at the top and note the change in the app's release notes.
