The business and the use case

CONSEQ is an iOS app for adults trying to quit a compulsive habit. The user restricts their own phone using Apple's Screen Time system and names one person — an "accountability contact" — who is notified if the user breaks the rules they set for themselves.

That notification is the only message this program sends. There is no marketing, no promotion, no newsletter, and no second message type. The app has no accounts, sells nothing, and shares no phone numbers with third parties other than the messaging provider that delivers the message.

Messages are sent by an automated service — the CONSEQ relay, a server application operated by the business — and are triggered by events the application detects. No message is composed or dispatched by one individual to another through this program; it is application-to-person messaging throughout.

There is exactly one opt-in path

A web consent form. Every person who receives a message from CONSEQ opted in by entering their own mobile number into the form linked above, ticking a box that is unticked by default, and then confirming by replying YES to a single confirmation text. There is no paper form, no verbal opt-in, no QR code, no purchased list, and no advertised keyword.

CONSEQ never sends the first message. An invited person hears about CONSEQ from someone they know, in an ordinary personal text sent from that person's own mobile handset through their own device's Messages app. CONSEQ is not the sender of that message, has no visibility into it, and no A2P traffic is involved. The first message CONSEQ itself sends goes only to a number that its owner typed into the consent form.

Step 1 — inside the app

The user requests an invite link

An existing CONSEQ user chooses one person they want to ask, and supplies their own first name. The app sends that first name — and nothing else — to the CONSEQ relay, which returns a single-use consent link. No phone number is transmitted to CONSEQ at this step, by design: the app is not permitted to hand the service a number belonging to somebody who has not agreed to anything.

The first name is required because the consent page must be able to say who is asking. A page that cannot name the requester is indistinguishable from a phishing link.

Step 2 — the user's own handset

The user texts the link personally

The app opens the iOS system message composer pre-filled with a personal message. The user sends it themselves, from their own mobile number, to whomever they choose. iOS provides no mechanism for an app to send a message on a user's behalf, and CONSEQ does not attempt one. If the user closes the composer without sending, nothing has happened at all.

The personal text — sent by the user, not by CONSEQ

hey — it's [Sam]. i'm using an app called CONSEQ to quit something, and it asks you to pick one person who finds out if i break my own rule. i picked you. this explains what you'd actually get, and you can say no — nothing happens unless you agree: [link]

This is person-to-person traffic on the sender's own consumer line. It deliberately carries no brand framing, no opt-out keyword instruction, and no rate disclosure, because those would dress a personal message up as traffic from a business.

Step 3 — the call to action

The invited person opens the link and reads the offer

The consent page identifies the program by name, names the person who asked, and states — above the form, without scrolling past anything — what would be received, how often, what it costs, how to stop, and where the policies are. It is reproduced in full at the preview URL above.

The page is server-rendered and requires no JavaScript, so it displays identically to any reviewer, on any device, with scripts disabled.

Step 4 — consent

They enter their own number and tick an unticked box

The invited person types their own mobile number and ticks a checkbox that is unticked by default. The form cannot be submitted without it. This is the consent language, verbatim, as it appears beside that checkbox:

I agree to receive text messages from CONSEQ at the number above, only when the person who invited me breaks a commitment they made — a few times a month at most. Msg & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to opt out, HELP for help.

The submission is recorded as the first half of the consent record: the mobile number, the exact wording above stored verbatim, the timestamp, and the submitting IP address and browser identifier.

Step 5 — confirmation

One confirmation text, and only then are they enrolled

CONSEQ sends exactly one message to the submitted number. A web form proves that somebody typed a number; it does not prove they hold that handset, and this closes that gap. Nothing further is ever sent unless they reply.

Confirmation message — the only message sent before enrolment

CONSEQ: You asked to be [Sam]'s accountability contact. Reply YES to confirm. You'd then get a text only when they break their own rules — a few times a month at most. Reply HELP for help, STOP to opt out at any time. Msg & data rates may apply.

Accepted affirmative replies: YES, Y, AGREE, ACCEPT. The inbound reply is recorded as the second half of the consent record — the phone number, the verbatim message body, the timestamp, and the carrier message SID.

Automatic reply confirming enrolment

CONSEQ: You're set. You'll only hear from us if the person who added you breaks their own rules — a few times a month at most. Reply HELP for help, STOP to opt out. Msg & data rates may apply.
Step 6 — after opt-in

What the recipient then receives

A short notification, only when the person who added them reports breaking a commitment they made, asks to disable the app's protections, or removes those protections outside the app. Every such message is signed and carries opt-out instructions:

it's me. I broke the protocol i set, [12] days into my run. you're the person i picked to find out. you don't have to do anything with this. i wanted it to cost something, and this is the cost. — sent via CONSEQ. Reply STOP to opt out.

Automatic replies

Sent by the program in response to a keyword, at any time, whether or not the recipient is enrolled.

Reply HELP or INFO

CONSEQ: You get a text only when the person who added you as their accountability contact breaks a commitment they made. Questions: ballast.ttt@gmail.com. Reply STOP to unsubscribe. Msg & data rates may apply.

Reply STOP (or any opt-out keyword)

You have successfully been unsubscribed. You will not receive any more messages from this number. Reply START to resubscribe.

The opt-out reply is issued by the Twilio Messaging Service at the carrier level, not by CONSEQ, so it takes effect even if our own systems are unreachable.

Reply YES from a number with no pending request

CONSEQ: There's no pending request for this number, so there's nothing to agree to and you won't receive messages from us.

Confirming such a reply would enrol somebody into a program they were never offered, so it is refused and the refusal is stated plainly.

Consent is active, never assumed

  • No pre-checked boxes exist anywhere in this flow. The consent checkbox is unticked, and the form cannot be submitted without it.
  • The recipient supplies their own number. No number reaches CONSEQ from any third party, including from the app user who invited them.
  • Silence is not consent. An invite link nobody opens expires after 48 hours and is deleted.
  • A number may be invited a maximum of 3 times, ever, across all users of the app. A single link accepts at most 3 submissions.
  • Replying NO or STOP is a permanent refusal. That number is never invited again by anyone and can never be messaged, no matter who submits it afterwards.
  • Consent is not transferable or assignable. It is stored against the phone number itself, applies only to this program and this sender, and cannot be granted by anyone other than the holder of that number.
  • The messaging service refuses, at the server, to send to any number without a recorded affirmative opt-in. This is enforced in code, not by policy alone.

What this program never does

  • No marketing or promotional messages of any kind.
  • No links or phone numbers in the body of any message CONSEQ sends.
  • No purchased, rented, or third-party-sourced phone number lists.
  • No mobile information — phone numbers, originator data, or SMS consent data — shared with or sold to any third party or affiliate for marketing or promotional purposes, ever.
  • No message to any number that has not submitted the consent form and replied with an opt-in keyword.
  • No message sent by CONSEQ to a number supplied by anyone other than its owner.
